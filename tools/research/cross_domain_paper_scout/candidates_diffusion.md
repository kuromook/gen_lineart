# Diffusion Track -- Candidates Log

Accumulating log of diffusion papers whose *mechanism* might address the
specific failure modes of this project's diffusion direction: condition
adherence (ControlNet output only loosely following the input rough),
over-inking, fine-tuning on a small paired dataset (~1.5k tiles), caption
conditioning, and two-stage structure-then-refine formulations. See
`README.md` for how this file gets populated and how to review it, and
`prompt_diffusion.md` for the full context handed to each run.

Topology/continuity candidates from non-art fields live in the separate
`candidates.md`.

Entries are grouped by the date/focus-topic of the run that found them.

## 2026-08-10 -- structure- and layout-preserving image-to-image diffusion (keeping input geometry, not just input semantics)

### A Tilted Seesaw: Revisiting Autoencoder Trade-off for Controllable Diffusion (2026)
- **Topic**: latent-space condition drift / VAE as a ceiling on controllability
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2601.21633 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21633)
- **Core idea**: Argues that autoencoders selected for generative quality (best gFID) rather than reconstruction quality induce "condition drift" in latent space, and that gFID is only weakly predictive of condition preservation while reconstruction-oriented (especially instance-level) metrics are strongly predictive. Proposes a multi-dimensional condition-drift evaluation protocol, including training lightweight predictors that try to recover the condition back out of the latent.
- **Relevance here**: Speaks directly to condition adherence, and possibly to over-inking: if the SD1.5 VAE cannot round-trip a binary thin-line tile, no amount of ControlNet training can recover geometry the latent never carried, and decode artifacts around thin strokes would inflate ink_ratio. Concretely: encode/decode the *ground-truth line tiles* through the frozen VAE with no diffusion at all and score F1@2px and ink_ratio of the reconstruction — that number is the hard ceiling for the whole diffusion track, and it is currently unmeasured.
- **Cost to try**: No training. A one-off evaluation script (VAE encode → decode → existing metrics). Hours, not GPU-days.
- **Confidence**: medium-high *as a diagnostic* (it is cheap and it either exonerates or indicts the latent space, which would redirect the whole track); low-medium as a *fix*, since the remedy would be swapping/finetuning the VAE, which is a much larger change.

### ControlNet-XS: Rethinking the Control of Text-to-Image Diffusion Models as Feedback-Control Systems (2023)
- **Topic**: control-adapter architecture / feedback bandwidth
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2312.06573 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06573)
- **Core idea**: Frames adapter-based control as a feedback-control system and diagnoses that in vanilla ControlNet the feedback from the generation process to the control branch is "timely sparse and has a small number of bits", so corrective signals arrive long after the features they should correct. The fix is architectural: rewire the control/generation communication to be high-frequency and large-bandwidth, which also lets the control branch be much smaller ("noticeably fewer parameters", ~2x faster train and inference).
- **Relevance here**: This is a *why-it-fails* account of exactly the observed symptom — the UNet commits to plausible content early and the control signal never pulls it back. It also happens to shrink the adapter, which matters a lot when a 361M-param adapter is being fit to 1,489 tiles; the retrained-10x result suggests the problem is not optimization but capacity/architecture mismatch. Concretely: retrain the adapter in the XS configuration instead of the standard ControlNet copy, at a fraction of the parameters.
- **Cost to try**: Architecture change plus a full retrain of the adapter (but a cheaper retrain than the current one).
- **Confidence**: medium. The diagnosis fits the symptom well and the smaller parameter count fits the data budget, but its results are demonstrated on depth/canny/segmentation over large datasets, not on a 1.5k-tile domain-specific pairing.

### ControlNet++: Improving Conditional Controls with Efficient Consistency Feedback (2024)
- **Topic**: training objective / explicit pixel-level condition consistency
- **Venue/link**: ECCV 2024, arXiv:2404.07987 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07987)
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that ControlNet optimizes controllability only *implicitly*, through the latent denoising loss, with no term that ever measures whether the output actually matches the condition (they show e.g. 27.46 vs 50.7 mIoU for segmentation control). Adds an explicit cycle-consistency reward: run a pretrained discriminative extractor on the generated image, compare the recovered condition to the input condition. To make this affordable they avoid backprop through the sampling chain by adding noise at a small timestep and doing a single-step denoise to get an image to reward (reported as taking memory from ~340GB to feasible).
- **Relevance here**: The project's loss is exactly the diagnosed one — `MSE(eps_hat, eps)` never sees the rough. The single-step-denoise reward trick is what makes this runnable on one GPU. Concretely: add an auxiliary reward on the single-step-decoded x0 against the ground-truth line tile using the metrics that are actually failing — a differentiable soft-F1/chamfer alignment term and an ink-ratio penalty — so over-inking and geometry drift are penalized directly rather than hoped for.
- **Cost to try**: Training-objective change plus a finetune of the existing adapter (does not require restarting from scratch).
- **Confidence**: medium. The mechanism attacks both failure modes head-on and this project has paired ground truth, so the "reward model" can be direct supervision rather than a noisy extractor. Risk: reward finetuning on 1.5k tiles could collapse diversity or over-thin strokes, and the paper's gains are on large-scale conditions.

### Heeding the Inner Voice: Aligning ControlNet Training via Intermediate Features Feedback (InnerControl) (2025)
- **Topic**: per-timestep alignment of intermediate UNet features to the control signal
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2507.02321 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02321)
- **Core idea**: Argues the previous fix (ControlNet++ above) only aligns at late denoising steps, while the image's structure is actually decided early. Trains lightweight convolutional probes that reconstruct the input control signal from intermediate UNet features at *every* denoising step, then uses those reconstructions as a per-step alignment loss across the whole trajectory.
- **Relevance here**: This is the sharpest available statement of the suspected mechanism — the layout is chosen in the early steps, which is precisely when a weakly-adhering adapter loses the rough's geometry, and it explains why inference-time conditioning-scale and guidance sweeps did nothing (they cannot undo a layout already committed). Concretely: train small probes to predict the rough tile from mid-block features at each step and add that as an auxiliary loss during adapter finetuning; even *before* changing the loss, the probes are an instrument — plot per-timestep recoverability of the rough to see exactly when the current model stops tracking it.
- **Cost to try**: Training change (extra probe networks + auxiliary loss), on top of a ControlNet++-style setup. The diagnostic-only version is cheap.
- **Confidence**: medium. Strong fit to the failure mode and the diagnostic use is low-risk, but it stacks on ControlNet++ so it inherits that complexity, and the paper is evaluated on standard large-scale control benchmarks.

### Plug-and-Play Diffusion Features for Text-Driven Image-to-Image Translation (2022/2023)
- **Topic**: training-free structure injection via UNet spatial features and self-attention
- **Venue/link**: CVPR 2023, arXiv:2211.12572 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12572)
- **Core idea**: Analyses (via PCA over UNet activations) *where* structure lives in a pretrained SD UNet: decoder layer-4 spatial features carry localized semantic layout shared across domains, and self-attention maps encode the spatial grouping. Translation is then done with no training at all — invert the guidance image, and during generation replace layer-4 features (up to timestep ~40/50) and decoder self-attention matrices (up to ~25/50) with those cached from the guidance image; the thresholds are lowered to 25/25 for primitive/textureless guidance images.
- **Relevance here**: Pure geometry preservation with zero data and zero training, i.e. the exact axis the diffusion track fails on, at the exact scale this project can afford. The "primitive/textureless guidance" caveat matters — a pencil rough *is* a textureless guidance image, and the paper already prescribes a different schedule for that case. Concretely: DDIM-invert the rough tile and inject its features into the anime-checkpoint sampling, and use the injection thresholds as a structure/style knob — a knob that is qualitatively different from the conditioning-scale and guidance-scale knobs already ruled out.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only. No retraining; can be tested against the existing eval harness in a day.
- **Confidence**: low-medium for a standalone fix — inverting an out-of-distribution pencil rough into an anime SD1.5 prior is a real domain gap, and PnP preserves layout but has no notion of producing binary ink. Still logged because the cost is near zero and because the layer/timestep analysis is reusable: it tells you which layers to touch in *any* of the trained approaches above.

## 2026-08-24 -- hallucination in conditional generation: measurement, diagnosis, and mitigation

### Tiled Prompts: Overcoming Prompt Misguidance in Image and Video Super-Resolution (2026)
- **Topic**: per-tile caption conditioning as a hallucination cause
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2602.03342 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03342), project page https://bryanswkim.github.io/tiled-prompts/
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that latent-tiling pipelines which condition every tile on one global caption suffer "prompt misguidance", split into errors of *omission* (the caption never mentions what is actually in this tile) and errors of *commission* (the caption asserts content that is not in this tile, so the prior invents it). The fix is to generate a tile-specific prompt per latent tile and sample under locally text-conditioned posteriors; inference-time, minimal overhead.
- **Relevance here**: This is the closest thing found to a named mechanism for the project's exact configuration — tile-based training with a single fixed caption for every tile — and "errors of commission" is a precise description of confidently generating plausible *different* content. It gives a reason to finally wire in the WD14 tags that already exist but are unused, and predicts *which* tiles should improve most (the ones whose content the global caption misdescribes).
- **Cost to try**: Data change first (attach the existing per-tile WD14 tags as captions), then an inference-time change; optionally a retrain with per-tile captions. The inference-only version on the current checkpoint is nearly free and is a clean ablation of the caption variable on its own.
- **Confidence**: medium. The setup match (tiles + one global caption) is unusually literal and the intervention is cheap, but the paper's task is super-resolution, where the LR image already pins the layout; here the caption may be carrying much more of the burden, so the effect could be larger *or* the ControlNet may simply be ignoring both signals. Worth running precisely because it isolates a variable the project has never varied.

### Tackling Structural Hallucination in Image Translation with Local Diffusion (2024)
- **Topic**: hallucination in image translation caused by local OOD regions of the condition
- **Venue/link**: ECCV 2024 (Oral), arXiv:2404.05980 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05980), code https://github.com/edshkim98/LocalDiffusion-Hallucination
- **Core idea**: Hypothesises and then tests that structural hallucination in conditional image translation comes from *local* out-of-distribution regions in the conditioning image, and that a local OOD region corrupts the prediction globally rather than just locally. Method: estimate OOD regions, run separate "branch" diffusion processes inside and outside them, then a "fusion" module merges the local predictions. Training-free, model-agnostic. Ablations attribute the gain specifically to OOD-localised generation and to segmentation accuracy.
- **Relevance here**: Speaks to condition adherence and, indirectly, over-ink: if a few unfamiliar pencil regions per tile (construction lines, smudges, hatching the training set never covered) are dragging the whole tile off the rough, that explains why the failure looks global and content-level while the cause would be local — and why 10x more steps did not help, since more steps do not make an OOD region in-distribution. The cheap first move is the diagnostic half only: estimate per-region OOD-ness of the rough tiles against the 1,489 training tiles and check whether tile-level F1@2px correlates with OOD area. If it does, the dataset-quality hypothesis gets concrete, and it says what to collect next rather than just "more data".
- **Cost to try**: Diagnostic is an evaluation-only script (an OOD estimator over the existing tiles, no training). The full method is an inference-time change with no retraining, but needs a working OOD segmenter for this domain, which is the real work.
- **Confidence**: low-medium. The diagnosis is the right *shape* for this failure and it is one of the few papers that studies hallucination in translation rather than in text-to-image, but the results are on medical and natural images where "OOD region" means a discrete lesion/object; a pencil rough may be diffusely OOD everywhere, in which case branching degenerates to the global case and buys nothing. Rated on the diagnostic value, not the fix.

### Understanding Hallucinations in Diffusion Models through Mode Interpolation (2024)
- **Topic**: hallucination measurement / self-detection from the sampling trajectory
- **Venue/link**: NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.09358 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09358)
- **Core idea**: Attributes hallucination to *mode interpolation*: when the true data distribution has disjoint modes, the learned score is a smooth approximation of what should be a step function between them, so sampling lands in between and produces content that exists in no training example. The measurement contribution is the useful part — the model "knows" when it has gone out of support, and this shows up as high variance in the sample's trajectory over the last few backward steps; a simple variance statistic over that trajectory removes >95% of hallucinations while keeping 96% of in-support samples.
- **Relevance here**: This is an instrument, not a fix: a per-tile hallucination score that needs no ground truth, computed from the sampling run itself. Concretely, score every eval tile by final-steps trajectory variance and regress it against the existing per-tile F1@2px. If they correlate, the project gets (a) confirmation that the drift is the model leaving its support rather than obeying a wrong prior, and (b) a reject-and-resample or flag-for-human loop on new roughs where no ground truth exists. If they do *not* correlate, that is itself informative and cheap: it means the outputs are on-manifold and merely wrong, which points at conditioning rather than at generation.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time instrumentation only — cache the x0 estimates across the last few steps of an already-working sampler and compute a variance. No training, no architecture change.
- **Confidence**: low-medium *as a fix*, medium *as a cheap disambiguating experiment*. The honest worry: this project's failures look like confident, well-formed, plausible anime ink — i.e. squarely on-manifold — which is exactly the case a support-violation detector is expected to miss. The paper's analysis is also on small synthetic/toy distributions, not SD1.5-scale conditional models. It is logged because the null result is as useful as the positive one and costs an afternoon.

### Unconditional Priors Matter! Improving Conditional Generation of Fine-Tuned Diffusion Models (2025)
- **Topic**: classifier-free guidance / the unconditional branch as a hallucination source
- **Venue/link**: WACV 2026, arXiv:2503.20240 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20240)
- **Core idea**: Observes that fine-tuning on a small dataset quietly degrades the model's *unconditional* noise prediction (it gets little of the limited training bandwidth), and that because CFG extrapolates along `uncond + s*(cond - uncond)`, a bad unconditional term corrupts every guided step no matter how good the conditional one is. Fix is inference-time and almost free: substitute the unconditional prediction from a base/other pretrained model while keeping the fine-tuned conditional prediction.
- **Relevance here**: Two distinct uses. (1) It applies literally to the parallel domain-only LoRA work — any LoRA fitted to ~1.5k tiles is exactly the small-data regime described, and this says to keep the base model's unconditional branch when sampling from it. (2) For the ControlNet setup the paper's own premise does *not* hold (the UNet is frozen, so its unconditional prior is intact), but the CFG decomposition it forces you to look at raises an unchecked question: is the ControlNet residual being applied to the unconditional pass as well as the conditional one? If it is, the control contribution largely cancels in `(cond - uncond)`, so the guidance scale amplifies only the text direction — which would explain both why guidance-scale sweeps did nothing for adherence and why a single fixed caption exerts so much influence over the content.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only, no retraining. The ControlNet-branch check is a one-line change to the sampler plus a re-run of the existing eval.
- **Confidence**: low-medium. Downgraded honestly: the paper's diagnosis does not describe the current frozen-UNet configuration, so this is logged for the adjacent experiment it suggests and for the LoRA track rather than for its own result. The cost is close to zero, which is the only reason it clears the bar.

## 2026-09-07 -- cross-attention and attention-map control for spatial faithfulness to a condition

### Training-Free Layout Control with Cross-Attention Guidance (2023)
- **Topic**: inference-time cross-attention manipulation for spatial layout adherence
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2304.03373 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03373), project page https://silent-chen.github.io/layout-guidance/
- **Core idea**: Directly manipulates the cross-attention layers that bind text tokens to image regions so that a token's attention map is pulled toward a user-specified region, with no training. Compares two strategies -- "forward" (directly editing attention scores) vs "backward" (computing a loss between the attention map and the target region, then backpropagating into the latent, i.e. a guidance gradient) -- and finds backward guidance clearly better.
- **Relevance here**: The project's caption is one fixed string for every tile, so whatever cross-attention the caption tokens produce is currently *unconstrained by the rough at all* -- ControlNet only adds residual features, it never touches where the text tokens are allowed to paint. This suggests a concrete, cheap, orthogonal lever that has not been tried: binarize the rough tile into a "content region" mask and use backward attention guidance to pull the caption tokens' cross-attention maps toward that mask at each sampling step. This is a genuinely different knob from the conditioning-scale/guidance-scale sweeps already ruled out, since those only scale existing signals rather than reshaping where attention is allowed to land.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only, no retraining -- add a per-step attention-map loss and backprop into the latent (backward guidance is more expensive per step than plain sampling but needs no new training data or model).
- **Confidence**: medium. The mechanism is a plausible, cheap first attack on caption content overriding the rough's silhouette, and it's untested here unlike the already-rejected scalar sweeps. Downgraded because the paper's layouts are discrete object boxes on natural-image SD, not a dense binary line-art mask, so the region-pooling attention loss may need retuning for this domain.

### Dense Text-to-Image Generation with Attention Modulation (DenseDiffusion) (2023)
- **Topic**: inference-time dense (per-pixel-region) attention modulation, no training
- **Venue/link**: ICCV 2023, arXiv:2308.12964 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12964)
- **Core idea**: Targets the case where captions are dense (a per-region description of the whole image, not one global caption for a single object) and directly modulates attention scores before softmax based on a layout map, rather than backpropagating a loss -- amplifying attention within a token's assigned region and suppressing it outside, with a normalization scheme to keep it stable. Matches purpose-trained layout-conditioned models without any training or extra data.
- **Relevance here**: This is arguably a closer literal fit than most layout-control papers, because the project's condition (a full-tile rough) is itself dense rather than a handful of object boxes -- the "region" for the whole caption could simply be "wherever the rough has ink," directly suppressing attention (and thus content generation) in blank regions of the tile. That is a direct, mechanism-level attack on both over-inking and content hallucination in blank/background areas, and it is inference-time so it is cheap to falsify.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only, no retraining -- requires deriving a binary region mask from the rough tile and patching the attention layers of the frozen UNet during sampling.
- **Confidence**: medium. Directly addresses over-ink via attention suppression outside the condition's inked area, which is a novel angle not covered by the conditioning-scale sweeps already tried; downgraded because the paper's masks come from user-specified object layouts with per-region sub-prompts, and adapting a single global caption to "attend everywhere the rough has ink, nowhere else" is a simplification that hasn't been validated for a dense dual-tone (ink/paper) domain.

### Diffusion Self-Guidance for Controllable Image Generation (2023)
- **Topic**: training-free guidance from a pretrained model's own attention maps and activations
- **Venue/link**: NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.00986 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00986)
- **Core idea**: Reads shape, position, size and appearance directly out of a pretrained diffusion model's cross-/self-attention maps and intermediate activations, then defines simple guidance terms (e.g. "this token's attention map should have this shape/centroid/size") that are optimized via gradient guidance during sampling -- no fine-tuning, no auxiliary model, no paired data.
- **Relevance here**: Offers a training-free way to impose exactly the kind of hard geometric constraint the ControlNet residual is failing to impose: extract the shape/position term from a "line" or "content" token's attention map and guide it toward the rough tile's silhouette at each step, as a self-contained diagnostic before committing to any retraining. Because it needs no paired data at all, it could also be tried directly on the domain-only rough/line generation work mentioned as a parallel track.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only, no retraining, no new data -- implemented as an extra guidance term in the existing sampler.
- **Confidence**: low-medium. The mechanism is well-suited to imposing shape/position constraints from attention, but the paper demonstrates it on natural-image objects with clear single-blob shapes, and a full anime line-art tile is a much denser, multi-part "shape" than the single-object cases shown; the guidance term may need substantial redesign for this domain.

### SketchKeyAnime: Reference-anchored Sparse Key-Sketch Animation Synthesis (2026)
- **Topic**: trained gating between structural (sketch) cross-attention and appearance/semantic conditioning
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2606.19958 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19958)
- **Core idea**: In a video diffusion model conditioned on both a reference appearance image and sparse key-sketches, introduces a dedicated "Sketch Cross Attention" branch with a *learnable gate* that explicitly balances how much the structural (sketch) signal vs. the appearance/semantic signal contributes at each denoising step, plus an adaptive loss that upweights line-art regions.
- **Relevance here**: This is the closest match found to the project's actual imbalance -- a single global caption's semantic/appearance influence currently seems to dominate a weakly-adhering structural (rough) signal, and this paper's fix is exactly a trained arbitration mechanism between those two channels rather than a fixed additive residual (as plain ControlNet uses) or a fixed scalar weight (as conditioning-scale already ruled out). Concretely: replace the ControlNet residual's unweighted addition with a learned per-step/per-location gate between the text cross-attention branch and a new sketch/rough cross-attention branch, and consider the paper's line-art-region-weighted loss as a way to directly discourage over-ink outside inked regions.
- **Cost to try**: Architecture change (adds a cross-attention branch and gate for the rough condition, rather than the current pure feature-residual ControlNet) plus a retrain of the adapter.
- **Confidence**: low-medium. The imbalance it targets matches this project's symptom unusually well, and it's one of the few papers found that treats structure-vs-appearance balance as a trained, per-step quantity rather than a fixed knob -- but it is designed for reference-image-driven video colorization with sparse keyframes, not single-tile static translation from a dense rough, and it requires the largest architecture change of the papers logged this round.

## 2026-09-21 -- guidance mechanisms beyond classifier-free guidance: condition strength, scheduling, and control-signal weighting

### SmartControl: Enhancing ControlNet for Handling Rough Visual Conditions (2024)
- **Topic**: spatially adaptive control-signal weighting based on detected text/condition conflict
- **Venue/link**: ECCV 2024, arXiv:2404.06451 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06451), project page https://smartcontrolnet.github.io/
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that a rough/imprecise visual condition often conflicts locally with what the text prompt implies, and that ControlNet's fixed, uniform conditioning-scale has no way to relax the condition only where it conflicts. The fix is a trained "control scale predictor" that outputs a *spatially varying* scale map from the degree of local text/condition conflict, so the model follows the rough tightly where it's trustworthy and defers to the prompt/prior only in the conflicting regions. Trained and validated with as few as ~1,000-2,000 paired samples.
- **Relevance here**: This is an unusually literal match to the project's own name for its input ("rough") and to its own already-tried, already-failed fix (a single global conditioning-scale scalar) -- it explicitly diagnoses why a *uniform* scale is the wrong lever and replaces it with a spatial map, which is a different axis than the scalar sweep already ruled out. It also demonstrates the mechanism works at almost exactly this project's dataset size (1,489 tiles vs. their 1,000-2,000). If content hallucination happens where the rough is genuinely ambiguous (faint construction lines, smudges) while confident strokes elsewhere are fine, a learned per-region scale map -- rather than one number -- is a plausible fix for both loose adherence and, indirectly, over-ink (regions with no trustworthy input signal would default toward "no ink" rather than invented content).
- **Cost to try**: Architecture + training change: add and train the control-scale-predictor module (their code is public) on top of the existing ControlNet setup; not a full retrain from scratch, but a new training run.
- **Confidence**: medium. The problem framing and data-scale both line up unusually well with this project's own setup, which is the main reason for the higher confidence than most inference-time-only entries in this log; downgraded from high because the paper's "conflict" signal is inferred from prompt-vs-mask disagreement in natural-image layouts, and adapting the conflict estimator to anime line-art/rough pairs is unvalidated.

### When ControlNet Meets Inexplicit Masks: A Case Study of ControlNet on its Contour-following Ability (Shape-aware ControlNet) (2024)
- **Topic**: adaptive modulation of contour-following strength based on estimated input-mask deterioration
- **Venue/link**: ACM MM 2024, arXiv:2403.00467 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00467), code https://github.com/DREAMXFAR/Shape-aware-ControlNet
- **Core idea**: Systematically studies how ControlNet's contour-following degrades as the input mask/condition becomes noisier or less precise ("inexplicit"), then adds a *deterioration estimator* that scores how corrupted a given input condition is, feeding that score into a shape-prior modulation block that adaptively turns contour-following up or down per input -- strong adherence for clean masks, more prior-driven generation for noisy ones, rather than one fixed adherence level for all inputs.
- **Relevance here**: A pencil rough sketch is exactly an "inexplicit mask" in this paper's sense -- it varies tile-to-tile in how clean vs. scribbly/ambiguous the strokes are, and the project currently applies one fixed adapter/conditioning-scale regardless. This paper's core empirical case study (measuring exactly how and why contour-following breaks down as input quality drops) is itself a useful diagnostic lens to apply to the existing eval set: bucket tiles by rough "cleanliness" and check whether F1@2px/ink_ratio failures concentrate in the messier tiles, before committing to retraining their full deterioration-estimator module.
- **Cost to try**: Diagnostic bucketing of existing tiles is evaluation-only. The full fix (deterioration estimator + shape-prior modulation block) is an architecture change plus a new training run.
- **Confidence**: medium for the diagnostic use (cheap, and it's the first paper found that treats "how messy is the input condition" as the explicit independent variable, which matches a real property of rough sketches this project has not measured); low-medium as a full fix, since it's validated on segmentation-style masks rather than sparse pencil strokes, where "deterioration" may look qualitatively different (missing/faint lines rather than blob noise).

### PixelPonder: Dynamic Patch Adaptation for Enhanced Multi-Conditional Text-to-Image Generation (2025)
- **Topic**: patch-level spatially-adaptive control selection + timestep-scheduled control injection
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2503.06684 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06684) -- appears to still be an arXiv preprint, no confirmed peer-reviewed venue found
- **Core idea**: For multi-condition ControlNet-like setups, diagnoses that separate fixed control branches "fight" during denoising and cause artifacts. Fixes this with two mechanisms: (1) a patch-level adaptive selection that dynamically decides, per sub-region, which control signal(s) are relevant there rather than applying all signals globally and uniformly; (2) a time-aware injection schedule that deliberately shifts control influence over the course of sampling -- weighted toward structural preservation in early/high-noise steps, then toward texture/appearance refinement in late/low-noise steps -- instead of a constant conditioning-scale across all timesteps.
- **Relevance here**: This project only has one control signal (the rough), so the multi-condition-conflict framing doesn't transfer directly, but mechanism (2) is a direct, concrete alternative to the flat per-run conditioning_scale already swept and ruled out: schedule the scale to be *highest* during the early steps that fix layout (consistent with the InnerControl finding already logged, that layout is decided early) and let it fall off later, rather than holding it constant. This is a cheap, orthogonal knob distinct from both the already-rejected scalar sweep and the heavier architectural entries above.
- **Cost to try**: Inference-time only if reusing the existing trained ControlNet with a per-timestep conditioning-scale schedule instead of a constant (the `diffusers` ControlNet pipeline already supports scheduling conditioning_scale by timestep); the patch-adaptive-selection half would need new training and does not apply with only one control signal.
- **Confidence**: low-medium. The timestep-scheduling half is nearly free to test and is a genuinely different knob from what's been ruled out, but the paper's actual reported gains are from the multi-condition patch-selection mechanism, which is inapplicable here with a single control signal -- so only a stripped-down piece of the method transfers.

## 2026-10-05 -- thin-structure and high-frequency detail loss in latent diffusion (VAE encode/decode bottleneck)

Note: the VAE-as-ceiling *diagnostic* is already logged above under 2026-08-10
("A Tilted Seesaw", arXiv:2601.21633) -- measure the frozen VAE's round-trip
F1@2px / ink_ratio on ground-truth line tiles first. The entries below are

### Designing a Better Asymmetric VQGAN for StableDiffusion (2023)
- **Topic**: decoder-side conditional branch that bypasses the latent bottleneck
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2306.04632 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04632), code https://github.com/buxiangzhiren/Asymmetric_VQGAN, and a `diffusers` implementation exists as `AsymmetricAutoencoderKL`
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that SD's vanilla VQGAN/VAE loses enough information in the 8x downsample + quantization that distortion appears even in image regions the diffusion model never touched, and that this hurts fine-grained structure most. Two changes, both decoder-side only: (1) a *conditional branch* that takes a task-specific pixel-space prior (in their case the unmasked region for inpainting) through a lightweight encoder and blends its features into the main decoder, giving high-resolution detail a direct path around the latent; (2) a decoder made much heavier than the encoder. Crucially the VAE encoder and the diffusion UNet are left unchanged -- only the decoder is retrained.
- **Relevance here**: This is the one mechanism found that attacks condition adherence and thin-stroke fidelity *at the same place*, and it does so without touching the frozen UNet or the ControlNet. The project already has, at decode time, exactly the kind of pixel-space prior the conditional branch wants: the rough tile. If the latent genuinely cannot carry 1px ink geometry, then stroke placement is being decided by information that no longer exists by the time the decoder runs, and a decoder that can *see the rough* can re-sharpen strokes onto the rough's actual stroke locations. Two concrete steps, cheap first: (a) drop in the released asymmetric decoder with no training and re-run the eval -- this tests "is a heavier decoder alone enough" for free; (b) if not, retrain only the decoder on the 1,489 pairs with the rough tile as the conditional-branch input, which is a far smaller fit than a 361M-param adapter and may also pull ink_ratio down, since a decoder conditioned on a mostly-white rough has a strong cue not to emit ink in blank areas.
- **Cost to try**: Step (a) is inference-time only (swap the decoder class). Step (b) is an architecture + training change, but confined to the decoder -- no diffusion retraining, and the decoder is trainable on the existing pairs or on line tiles alone.
- **Confidence**: medium. Highest of this round because the UNet and adapter stay frozen (so it composes with, rather than replaces, the one thing that actually worked -- crisp binary ink), the parameter count is modest, and the free version is a one-line test. Downgraded from high because their conditional prior is a large *known-exact* image region (inpainting, where the answer is literally handed to the decoder) whereas a rough is a hint, not the answer; and because if the Tilted-Seesaw round-trip test shows the VAE reconstructs line tiles fine, this whole entry is moot.

### Latent Wavelet Diffusion For Ultra-High-Resolution Image Synthesis (2026)
- **Topic**: frequency-aware reweighting of the denoising loss + scale-consistent VAE objective
- **Venue/link**: ICLR 2026, arXiv:2506.00433 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00433)
- **Core idea**: Two separable mechanisms. (1) A *scale-consistency* term added to the VAE objective, `||D(E(x_down)) - x_down||^2`, penalising disagreement between full-resolution and downsampled reconstruction, which they argue suppresses high-frequency artifacts and raises the spectral fidelity of the latent. (2) A training-time, parameter-free saliency mask: take a single-level DWT of the *latent*, sum LH/HL/HH energy per location into a map `A` normalised to [0,1], and mask the denoising loss with `M_t(i,j) = 1 iff T*(A(i,j) + 0.3) >= t`, so high-frequency locations receive supervision over many more training steps than flat ones. No architecture change, zero inference overhead.
- **Relevance here**: The project's loss is a flat `MSE(eps_hat, eps)` over the whole latent, which means a tile that is ~90% blank paper spends ~90% of its gradient on getting blank regions right -- a loss that is nearly indifferent to whether the thin strokes landed in the right place is a plausible mechanical reason the model scores 0.20 F1 while looking confident, and it is a reason that *more steps would not fix*, which matches the rejected 10x-run result. Concretely: compute per-tile wavelet-energy (or just dilated ground-truth ink) masks and reweight the existing adapter's MSE loss toward stroke-adjacent latent positions. This is a small code change to the existing training loop, not a new pipeline, and it is a strictly different intervention from the per-region *scale* map in SmartControl (logged 2026-09-21) -- that reweights the condition, this reweights the loss.
- **Cost to try**: Training-objective change, finetune of the existing adapter from its current checkpoint (not a restart). The scale-consistent VAE half is a separate, optional VAE finetune and can be skipped on the first pass.
- **Confidence**: medium for the loss-masking half, low for the VAE half. The masking mechanism transfers cleanly to eps-prediction and to this project's scale, and the diagnosis (uniform loss over a sparse-signal image) is specific and checkable. Honest caveats: their gains are reported at 2K-4K on Flux/SD3/SANA where "detail-rich region" means texture, not sparse ink on white, and all their numbers come from A100 runs on 4K datasets; also, upweighting ink regions could plausibly make over-inking *worse* before it makes geometry better, so ink_ratio must be watched alongside F1.

### Sketch2Manga: Shaded Manga Screening from Sketch with Diffusion Models (2024)
- **Topic**: VAE decoder finetuning for a high-frequency, non-natural-image domain
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2403.08266 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08266)
- **Core idea**: A two-stage sketch -> colour illustration -> screentoned manga pipeline, but the part that matters here is the VAE finding: the original SD VAE decoder produces distorted, blurry screentones, which they attribute to information loss in latent encoding plus the decoder never having seen manga. They finetune the decoder following the `sd-vae-ft-mse` recipe (adversarial + MSE + LPIPS) and report an ablation that **LPIPS actively degrades** high-frequency reconstruction here, which they attribute to the domain shift between manga and natural images; dropping LPIPS significantly improves screentone quality.
- **Relevance here**: Screentones and 1px ink lines are the same problem -- near-Nyquist binary structure that a natural-image-trained VAE smooths. This is independent empirical confirmation, in the project's own art domain, that the SD VAE decode step *is* a real ceiling for exactly this kind of content, which raises the prior on the Tilted-Seesaw round-trip test coming back bad. The LPIPS ablation is the directly transferable prescription: if the project finetunes the SD1.5 VAE decoder on its line tiles, use MSE + adversarial and leave LPIPS out -- which is the opposite of the default advice and is the kind of detail that would otherwise cost a wasted run to discover. Decoder finetuning also needs only *line images*, not pairs, so it draws on the plentiful-unpaired-data situation the parallel domain-only track is already exploiting, rather than on the 1,489 pairs.
- **Cost to try**: A separate, self-contained training run for the VAE decoder only (encoder, UNet and ControlNet all untouched and reusable afterwards). Data change is nil -- reuses existing line tiles.
- **Confidence**: medium as a *prescription* (the no-LPIPS finding is concrete, cheap to honour, and comes from the right domain), low-medium as a *fix* on its own: their decoder finetune used ~289k manga images, roughly two orders of magnitude more than this project has, and a decoder overfitted on ~1.5k tiles could introduce its own artifacts. Note also that this fixes decode sharpness only -- it cannot recover stroke geometry the encoder already threw away, so it pairs naturally with the Asymmetric-VQGAN entry above rather than competing with it.

### Edge-preserving noise for diffusion models (2026)
- **Topic**: anisotropic, edge-aware forward noise process
- **Venue/link**: Eurographics 2026 / Computer Graphics Forum 45(2), arXiv:2410.01540 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01540), project page https://edge-preserving-diffusion.mpi-inf.mpg.de/
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that standard isotropic Gaussian noise destroys edge structure at the same rate as flat regions, so the model gets no training signal that distinguishes "where the boundary is" from "what fills the region". Replaces it with an anisotropic noise model inspired by Perona-Malik anisotropic diffusion -- noise is suppressed along high-gradient directions, so edges survive to higher noise levels -- plus an edge-aware scheduler that transitions from edge-preserving to isotropic noise over the trajectory. Works in both diffusion and flow-matching formulations, and they note an existing isotropic model can be *finetuned* into the scheme rather than trained from scratch. Demonstrated specifically on stroke-to-image synthesis.
- **Relevance here**: It is the cleanest why-account found for the "structure is decided early and the condition cannot pull it back" story already logged (InnerControl, 2026-08-10): if boundary geometry is the first thing the forward process erases, then the early, layout-deciding steps are precisely the steps with the least edge information available, and the model has little choice but to fall back on the prior -- i.e. generate plausible different content. Stroke-to-image being their headline application is a notably close task match.
- **Cost to try**: High and awkward. The noise process is a property of the *denoiser*, so honouring it means finetuning the frozen SD1.5 UNet, which forfeits the anime prior that is the only thing currently working. It is also formulated on pixel-space gradients, and "edges" of an 8x-downsampled 4-channel latent are not obviously the same object. A faithful test is effectively a new model, not a modification of this one.
- **Confidence**: low. Logged for the diagnosis, not the method -- it is a reason to expect that *no* adapter-side fix fully closes the geometry gap under isotropic noise, and a useful argument to have on file if the ControlNet route keeps failing and the question becomes whether to train something small from scratch in pixel space. Explicitly flagged as a trap otherwise: it is the sort of plausible-sounding mechanism that would consume a long training run, and this project has already paid that bill once.

Note on scope: the entries below are about the *input side* -- what the rough is
turned into before the adapter sees it, and what the training distribution
teaches the model that input means. That is a different axis from the entries
already logged about how strongly the condition is *applied* (SmartControl's

### CtrLoRA: An Extensible and Efficient Framework for Controllable Image Generation (2024/2025)
- **Topic**: condition embedding network initialisation + reusable base adapter
- **Venue/link**: ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.09400 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09400), code and weights https://github.com/xyfJASON/ctrlora
- **Core idea**: Two mechanisms, both bearing on how the condition enters the model. (1) A *diagnosis of ControlNet's condition embedding network*: the small randomly-initialised conv stack that maps the 512px condition image down to 64px "cannot extract any useful information from the condition image at the beginning of training", so the adapter spends early training learning to see its own input; they replace it with the frozen pretrained SD VAE encoder, which already emits meaningful features and whose output space matches what the Base ControlNet (itself a trainable copy of the SD encoder) expects. They explicitly re-attribute the "sudden convergence phenomenon" partly to this random init rather than solely to zero-convolutions, and report that with the VAE embedder the sudden jump is replaced by oscillation around a local minimum between ~2k-3k steps. (2) A Base ControlNet (~360M params) trained jointly over 9 base conditions to hold condition-agnostic image-to-image knowledge, after which a *new* condition is learned as ~37M rank-128 LoRA params plus norm layers and zero-convs.
- **Relevance here**: This is the most directly actionable entry in the log, for three reasons. First, it is the only paper found that treats the condition *encoder* as the suspect -- the project has swept how hard the condition is pushed and how long it is trained, but has never questioned whether its 4-layer random-init encoder resolves 1px pencil strokes at all after the 8x downsample. Second, it reframes the hypothesis the project already spent a 10x run rejecting: "undertrained" was tested by adding steps, but CtrLoRA's claim is that the failure is in *where training starts*, which more steps does not fix -- consistent with F1 moving only 0.202 -> 0.216. Third, the data budget matches almost exactly: ~1,000 pairs and <1 hour on a single consumer GPU to adapt to a new condition (their table lists ~0.17 GPU-hours for a Lineart condition), against 1,489 tiles here, and fitting 37M params instead of 361M is a far better match to the dataset size. It is SD1.5-based, the released Base ControlNet is `ctrlora_sd15_basecn700k.ckpt`, the repo explicitly supports SD1.5 community checkpoints including anime ones, and the expected data layout is `source/` + `target/` + `prompt.json`, i.e. what the project already has.
- **Cost to try**: Architecture + training change, but the cheapest retrain in the log: download the released Base ControlNet, point the finetune script at the existing 1,489 pairs, train a rank-128 LoRA. No base pretraining needed (theirs cost ~6,000 GPU-hours on 20M pairs -- do not reproduce it). Note the script's default `--max_steps` is 100k, far above the claimed one hour, so cap it.
- **Confidence**: medium-high -- the highest in this log so far, and the reasoning is budget-fit rather than paper strength: it is a near-drop-in replacement that attacks an unexamined component, shrinks the fitted parameter count by 90% on a dataset that is 1.5k samples, and reuses released SD1.5 weights. Downgraded from high because the Base ControlNet's 9 base conditions are natural-image conditions (canny/HED/depth-style), so "condition-agnostic image-to-image knowledge" may transfer poorly to pencil-rough -> anime-ink; and because using the VAE as the condition embedder inherits whatever thin-stroke information the VAE throws away, which is exactly the quantity the 2026-10-05 round-trip test is supposed to measure. Run that test first -- if the VAE cannot round-trip a line tile, the VAE-as-embedder half of this is self-defeating and only the LoRA half is worth keeping.

### AirSketch: Generative Motion to Sketch (2024)
- **Topic**: self-supervised synthesis of degraded control signals from clean targets
- **Venue/link**: NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2407.08906 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08906)
- **Core idea**: The task is noisy-strokes-in, clean-sketch-out (hand-tracking traces from air drawing -> a clean sketch), and the mechanism is that they never collect a single real pair. For each clean sketch `x0` they apply random augmentations `A` and feed `A(x0)` as the ControlNet condition while the denoising loss targets `x0`, so the adapter learns spatial conditioning and noisy->clean mapping simultaneously. The augmentations are organised into three named families, each applied with p=0.5: *local* (per-stroke wave distortion, spikes, jitter along strokes), *structural* (whole-sketch shrink/aspect/reposition, per-stroke misplacement or resize -- mutually exclusive, since both at once destroys the sketch), and *false strokes* (transitional lines drawn between strokes, plus random extra lines on the canvas). Their ablation separates the effects: local augmentation mostly buys local similarity (SSIM 0.55 -> 0.60), false strokes mostly buy *global* similarity (Chamfer distance -9%), structural is weakest (~3% CD), and a ControlNet trained with no augmentation "fails to improve on the raw tracking input" at all.
- **Relevance here**: This is the clearest mechanism found for the small-paired-dataset failure mode, and it points at material the project already has in quantity. The project has 1,489 real pairs but plentiful unpaired line art (the domain-only track is already exploiting that asymmetry); this says to manufacture pseudo-roughs *from the clean line tiles* and train on tens of thousands of synthetic pairs, with the real 1,489 as a smaller high-fidelity set. The false-stroke family is the interesting part for this project specifically: "transitional lines between strokes" and "random extra lines on the canvas" are a good caricature of pencil construction lines, overdraw and hatching, and theirs is the family their ablation credits with the *global/Chamfer* gain -- which is the axis F1@2px measures. It also reframes the problem usefully: the project is currently asking the adapter to learn rough->line from 1,489 examples of a mapping, when the inverse (line -> plausible rough) is cheap to simulate.
- **Cost to try**: Data change plus a retrain. The augmentation code is pure image/stroke processing with no model involved, so the pipeline is buildable and inspectable before any GPU time; the retrain is then the same shape as the current one, just with far more (synthetic) data. Composes with CtrLoRA above rather than competing -- synthetic pairs are exactly what a 37M-param LoRA wants more of.
- **Confidence**: medium. The mechanism is simple, the ablation is quantified per-family, and it removes the binding constraint (paired data) rather than working around it. Honest caveats: their clean targets are Quick, Draw! doodles -- a handful of strokes -- not dense anime line art, so a synthetic degradation that looks convincing on a doodle may be visibly unlike a real pencil rough on a complex tile, and the whole thing rests on the synthetic degradations covering the real rough distribution (the standard sim-to-real risk; the fix if it underperforms is to fit the degradation parameters to the 1,489 real pairs rather than guessing them). They used SDXL on 2xH100 with ~60k sketches, so the reported numbers are out of reach, but the mechanism itself is scale-free. Partially overlaps the degraded-mask training noted in Shape-aware ControlNet (logged 2026-09-21), which found degradation augmentation raises robustness but *weakens* contour-following -- a direct warning that over-aggressive augmentation could push adherence the wrong way, so the augmentation strength needs to be an ablated knob, not a fixed recipe.

### It's All About Your Sketch: Democratising Sketch Control in Diffusion Models (2024)
- **Topic**: sketch abstraction level as an explicit conditioning variable; abstraction-driven timestep sampling
- **Venue/link**: CVPR 2024 (pp. 7204-7214), arXiv:2403.07234 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07234)
- **Core idea**: A pilot study attributes the deformed, edge-bleeding outputs of ControlNet/T2I-Adapter/SGDM on freehand sketches to spatial conditioning plus the fact that those models are trained on *synthetic edgemaps rather than real amateur sketches*; supporting observations are that the best sketch-guidance weight differs per sketch and that a null prompt badly distorts outputs. Three mechanisms follow. (1) A *sketch adapter* that abandons spatial conditioning entirely: a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 patch embedding (257x1024) is mapped to a 77x768 sequence and fed to SD's cross-attention in place of a text prompt, so no text is needed at inference. (2) *Adaptive time-step sampling*: a CLIP sketch classifier trained with a MagFace-style loss yields an abstraction score `a` in [0,1] as the L2 norm of the sketch feature (near 1 = edgemap-like, near 0 = highly abstract); with `w = clip(1-a, 0.2, 0.8)`, timesteps are drawn from `S_w(t) = (1/T)(1 - w*cos(pi*t/T))`, so more abstract sketches are trained at *larger* t -- the early, high-noise steps where high-level structure is set. (3) Discriminative guidance: Tweedie's formula gives a clean latent from the noisy one, the frozen VAE decodes it to `x0_hat`, and the loss is `1 - cos` between a frozen FG-SBIR model's features of the input sketch and of `x0_hat`.
- **Relevance here**: Mechanism (2) is the transferable one and it is a knob the project has never touched: the adapter is currently trained with uniform timestep sampling, and if layout is decided early (the InnerControl story, logged 2026-08-10) then uniform sampling spends most of its gradient on steps that cannot fix layout. Concretely: bias the training timestep distribution toward high t, and -- the more interesting version -- compute a per-tile abstraction/roughness score and make the bias per-tile, so scribbly tiles get trained predominantly on the structure-deciding steps. This is a change to a sampler inside the existing training loop, not a new pipeline. The paper's diagnosis also lands on this project: ControlNet checkpoints and conditioning conventions come from synthetic edgemaps, while the input here is genuine pencil, which is the distribution gap AirSketch above proposes to close synthetically. Mechanism (3) is the same single-step-x0 reward trick already logged under ControlNet++ (2026-08-10), so treat it as independent corroboration that the trick works, not as a new idea.
- **Cost to try**: Mechanism (2) is a training change only (replace uniform `t ~ U(0,T)` with their `S_w`), finetunable from the current checkpoint; the per-tile abstraction score needs a roughness proxy, and a crude one (stroke-density, or ink-ratio of the rough, or rough-vs-line ink ratio) is probably enough to start without training a MagFace classifier. Mechanism (1) is an architecture change and should be *skipped*.
- **Confidence**: medium for the timestep-sampling half, low for the rest, and the split matters. The timestep schedule is cheap, orthogonal to everything already ruled out, and has a mechanistic story that lines up with two other entries in this log. But mechanism (1) is a trap for this project and worth stating plainly: routing the sketch through a 77x768 cross-attention sequence deliberately *discards* spatial precision, and their notion of fidelity is instance-level retrieval similarity on single-object photos -- that can look good to FG-SBIR while scoring near-zero on F1@2px, which is the metric that actually has to move here. Their data (Sketchy, ~12.5k photos with >=5 sketches each) is also an order of magnitude past this project's pairs.

### Block and Detail: Scaffolding Sketch-to-Image Generation (2024)
- **Topic**: what blank regions of the condition *mean*, as set by the training pair construction
- **Venue/link**: arXiv:2402.18116 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18116) -- ACM publication record consistent with UIST 2024, venue not confirmed from search
- **Core idea**: Diagnoses that a standard ControlNet trained on *complete* edgemaps learns "no edge here" to mean "no detail in the image here", which makes it useless for an in-progress sketch. The fix is entirely in the data: extract an edge map, vectorise it into strokes, estimate a plausible drawing order by ranking strokes by distance from the foreground/background boundary, then delete the last X% of strokes with X random -- training on (partial sketch, complete image) pairs at many completion levels, which teaches the model that a stroke-free region is *not-yet-specified* and should be plausibly filled. Notably the condition itself does not encode the blocking/detail distinction at all -- the two stroke sets are OR'd into one map (`b (+) d`) for the adapter; the distinction is used only at inference, to build renoising masks: dilate blocking strokes by a large radius, detail strokes by a small one, and renoise `mask_b AND NOT mask_d` (SDEdit/Blended Latent Diffusion at t=0.8), so regions near precise strokes are frozen and regions near loose strokes are free to change.
- **Relevance here**: Logged mainly as an inverted diagnosis, and it is a sharp one: this paper *deliberately engineers* the behaviour the project is complaining about. If a model trained on partial conditions invents plausible content in blank regions, then the question for this project is what its own 1,489 pairs teach about rough-ink-to-line-ink correspondence -- and a pencil rough is not a subset of its clean line art, it is a messy superset with many strokes the inker *discards*. That makes the training signal "rough ink here may or may not become line ink, and line ink may appear where the rough was faint", which is a textbook weak/ambiguous condition and a plausible mechanical reason for both loose adherence and 6x over-ink that no amount of extra steps repairs. The cheap first move needs no model at all: measure, over the training set, the per-pixel correspondence between dilated rough ink and line ink -- precision/recall of rough-ink as a predictor of line-ink. If recall is high but precision is low (lots of rough ink dropped by the inker), the condition is intrinsically ambiguous and the dataset, not the architecture, is the bottleneck. Separately, the stroke-role dilation masks suggest a usable inference-time trick in the *opposite* direction: freeze (blend back) regions far from any rough stroke toward white instead of letting them be generated, which attacks over-ink directly on the existing checkpoint.
- **Cost to try**: The correspondence measurement is an evaluation-only script over existing data -- an afternoon, no GPU. The inverted renoising/blending trick is inference-time only on the current checkpoint. Their actual method (partial-sketch training data) is a data change, and is probably *not* wanted here, since this project needs blank to mean blank.
- **Confidence**: low-medium, and honestly rated: nothing in the paper is a fix for this project, and its goal is the exact opposite of what is needed. It clears the bar on the strength of the diagnostic it implies -- rough/line ink correspondence is a property of the dataset that has never been measured here, is free to measure, and would either implicate or exonerate the "dataset size/quality" suspect that is currently being carried on intuition. Their scale (~45k LAION-Art images, SD1.5, 2xV100) is irrelevant to the diagnostic use. Downgraded because single-object-centric natural photos with vectorisable edge maps are a long way from anime tiles, so their specific stroke-ordering heuristic would not transfer even if wanted.
